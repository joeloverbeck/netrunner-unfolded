# Bot design and improvement

Research checked **2026-11-14**. This note supports the next-specification decision: complete official introductory matches, with 30 Runner cards and 34 Corp cards, either human role, save/resume, and basic opponents. Jon accepted that delivery boundary and separately required that bots **never receive information unavailable to their player in a real game**. Experienced-casual strength remains the larger target. The accepted content counts and rules authority are in [rules and content](../foundation/02-rules-and-content.md); product constraints are in [mission and play](../foundation/01-mission-and-play.md).

This is a source investigation and a proposed technical direction, not implementation, readiness, training authorization, or measured bot strength. No matches, training jobs, benchmarks, or native play were run. The present [engine at `0b1535c`](https://github.com/joeloverbeck/netrunner-unfolded/blob/0b1535c89f41450fd345f3b46f781c2c4a201ac7/src/game.rs) implements the finite Brân position; it cannot yet produce full-game training or evaluation data. GitHub owns subsequent work and acceptance; the stages below are conditional alternatives, not a second work queue.

The decision is how little bot machinery is needed for the first complete game, while keeping a credible path toward better play and more supported decks. The incumbent is a fixed Runner continuation in the bounded run. The least-complex credible next opponent is a separate hand-authored policy for each role, with exact calculations for supported tactics. The recommendation is to build that comparator before information-set search or learning, and make every later mechanism earn its cost against it. This research consulted `PURP-1`–`PURP-6`, `WORK-1`–`WORK-6`, `EVID-1`–`EVID-10`, `SEM-1`–`SEM-2`, `REP-1`–`REP-3`, `BOUND-1`–`BOUND-5`, `INT-1`–`INT-4`, `AGENT-1`–`AGENT-5`, `OPS-1`–`OPS-7`, and `GOV-1`–`GOV-3`.

## Recommended first delivery

Use **one rules engine, two role-specific policies, lawful remembered information, bounded tactical planning, and small diagnostic records**. Corp and Runner can share decision scheduling, sampling utilities and measurement code where actually useful. Their plans, features and evaluations should differ. No universal card-game framework, model trainer, general card language, or full-game equilibrium solver is needed for this delivery.

Gameplay stays offline without an LLM. Any later project-owned batch, analysis or training executable is Rust and runs locally within an expressly selected budget. The research implementations cited below are evidence, not selected runtime dependencies.

| Policy | Useful initial decisions | Mistakes the baseline should expose clearly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corp | Keep enough money to rez relevant defenses; develop economy; protect useful servers; install and advance agendas with a scoring plan; select affordable rez responses; weigh a trap against its cost and likely access. | Advancing with no viable completion plan; spending the last defensive credits casually; leaving immediate public wins undefended when an affordable response exists; predictable installations that distinguish every agenda from every trap. |
| Runner | Build economy and breaker coverage; compare useful run targets; calculate supported break/payment sequences; preserve resources for later ICE and access; weigh damage risk against hand size, tempo and score pressure. | Spending all credits on one encounter then being unable to finish; paying to break a harmless subroutine automatically; refusing every unknown server; running every unknown server; valuing access only by immediate agenda probability. |

These are **design recommendations**, not observed competence. Start with a modest number of explicit plan choices such as develop, pressure a central, contest a remote, or score. Re-evaluate when a new observation invalidates the plan. A planned sequence never authorizes skipping an intervening opponent choice or engine timing window. Card-specific advice can rank actions; only the engine can make them legal.

Exact local calculations are a better first investment than long random playouts: affordable breaker lines, restricted credits, click costs, remaining subroutines and immediate public outcomes have concrete independent expectations. Preserve several materially different costs rather than declaring the lowest credit cost universally best. Sacrificing Mayfly, losing cards, consuming a click, or spending run-only credits can change which line is desirable. An exact calculation over known information is lawful; a calculation silently using unrezzed ICE identities is not.

The introductory cards already challenge a fixed deck script: Unity's strength depends on installed icebreakers; Carmen's installation depends on successful-run history; Red Team tracks attacked centrals; Tread Lightly changes rez costs; Overclock's hosted credits differ from the pool tested by Whitespace; Manegarm creates a late click-or-credit choice. These are source-derived reasons to compose supported mechanics and small tactical hooks rather than memorize a sequence for one deck. The current [official card snapshot](https://github.com/Null-Signal-Games/netrunner-cards-json/blob/6ff880724741ea6ac062acf77e0cf979bb44e67e/pack/sg.json) supplies the text; resolving all interactions still requires the selected rules and rulings.

The basic opponent should make legal choices throughout the selected decks, reach rules-defined ends without getting stuck, preserve its knowledge across save/resume, and satisfy the information boundary. It need not satisfy an invented win-rate threshold to count as basic. Finishing games does not establish experienced-casual strength.

## The information boundary is a hard requirement

**Verified rules:** CR 26.03 distinguishes private looking from revealing; draw decks are hidden from both players; hands are private to their owners; and installing a card does not normally reveal its type. The Brân example explicitly preserves concealment. These rules invalidate conveniences such as giving Corp its own future draws or informing Corp of a card privately accessed from R&D. See [CR §§1.21.2–1.21.6, 4.2.2, 4.3.2 and 8.5.13](https://rules.nullsignal.games/).

**Proposed policy input:** the current player observation, lawful action descriptions, that player's earlier observations and choices, and permitted pregame knowledge. The known introductory lists can be used because this teaching configuration declares them. Future selection of an opponent deck by setup code does not make its exact list public. A closed-deck mode must use a declared prior over possible decks rather than secretly reading the selected list.

OpenSpiel provides a useful established distinction: an information state includes lawful remembered history, whereas a current observation may omit earlier knowledge. Its contract requires consistency with each player's action–observation history and permits different hidden worlds to yield the same player information state ([`spiel.h`, revision `4956843`, lines 589–627 and 672–687](https://github.com/google-deepmind/open_spiel/blob/4956843d7375e29a4c986425c66f897d7ac15340/open_spiel/spiel.h#L589-L687)). This is an interface precedent, not an adopted dependency or verification of our engine.

Knowledge needs more precision than a visible/hidden flag:

- Own hand and permitted inspections are available; own draw order and opponent hand are not.
- Counts, public resources, visible installed cards and public events can constrain possibilities. A remembered private look is available only to the player who received it.
- A known card entering a shuffled deck remains part of composition knowledge, but loses its known position. Movement into an indistinguishable hand can preserve a count or possibility without preserving a trackable object identity.
- Opponent actions support uncertain inference. “They did not rez, therefore this cannot be expensive ICE” is not a rules fact. A weak or unconventional opponent can choose unexpectedly.
- Save/resume must restore legitimate memory and policy randomness, or reconstruct them from a retained player-view prefix. It must not rebuild the bot's knowledge by inspecting the current omniscient save.

The authoritative controller necessarily knows hidden state to enforce rules. The bot must not receive that state, a reference to it, a live-state query callback, the real gameplay RNG, or privileged cached features. Hiding card faces is insufficient: action counts, target ordering, descriptions, hashes, stable internal IDs, debug messages and candidate-pruning decisions can reveal secrets. Even an unavailable option can leak a card's existence if its appearance depends on concealed information. Derive the decision interface from the acting player's information rights and the governing rules.

For a future searcher, create a **hypothetical world from allowed information**. Do not clone the real match and merely promise not to inspect selected fields. Sampling must use separate randomness, preserve lawful constraints and leave actual gameplay chance untouched. In simulated continuations, each actor receives its own simulated observations and remembered history. The simulator may know sampled secrets to resolve effects; the policy acting within it may not.

Proposed verification changes hidden facts in pairs of legal worlds that are indistinguishable to the acting player. With equal lawful histories, policy seeds and fixed work, the full inputs and selected actions should agree. Cover both roles, private looks, unknown hand cards, hidden deck permutations, shuffles, facedown movement, action ordering and resumed games. A second family deliberately changes a legitimate observation and checks that the input can change. This tests both accidental disclosure and accidental removal of useful knowledge. Source-derived examples and code review remain necessary: a few paired fixtures cannot prove that all disclosures are absent.

## Search choices and their limits

The important distinction is between **choosing with uncertainty** and using a sequence of perfect-information answers as if the player could know which answer applies.

**Strategy fusion:** suppose two facedown remotes exchange agenda and trap identities without changing what Runner knows. A planner cannot credit itself with running the agenda in both worlds. Nor can a rollout choose different later actions before any distinguishing observation. Aggregate the consequences of a single lawful contingent policy across sampled worlds. Private information revealed later can legitimately change that policy's later action.

**Nonlocality:** in a hidden-information game, decisions in one apparent continuation can depend on strategies and reach probabilities elsewhere in the game. Cutting out a local subtree and assigning ordinary state values can discard those dependencies. In Netrunner, the credibility of an installation as a bluff depends partly on how agendas and non-agendas are played elsewhere. This is a reasoned application, not a measured Netrunner result. Lisý, Lanctot and Bowling give a formal example and show that ISMCTS can become more exploitable with additional computation in their test games ([OOS, §§2.2 and 4](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/aamas15-iioos.pdf)). More search is not inherently better strategy.

| Method | Verified basis | Recommended disposition here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hand-authored policy plus bounded tactical enumeration | No learning premise is needed; calculations can be checked against rules-derived positions. | First complete-match baseline. Keep the policy replaceable and its limitations visible. |
| Sampled short-plan comparison with fixed lawful continuation policies | Estimates consequences under the sampled beliefs and those continuation policies. | First search experiment after the baseline. Compare the same root candidates on the same sampled worlds; retain uncertainty and cutoff reasons. This is model-dependent planning, not equilibrium play. |
| ISMCTS | Shares statistics across information sets; variants account for partially observed moves and multiple observers. | Candidate when fixed-policy planning has a measured weakness. Choose the observer model explicitly; a single root-view tree is not sufficient evidence of correct opponent knowledge. [Cowling et al., §§IV-E–IV-G](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/75048/1/CowlingPowleyWhitehouse2012.pdf). |
| POMCP | Uses a history tree, sampled hidden states and particle beliefs with a generative simulator. Its stated convergence assumes the appropriate belief and POMDP model. | Useful conceptual model for planning against a specified opponent policy. An adaptive adversary does not automatically become a stationary environment. Particle depletion and a misspecified opponent remain practical risks. [Silver and Veness, §§2–4](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2010/file/edfbe1afcf9246bb0d40eb4d8027d90f-Paper.pdf). |
| CFR / sampled CFR / OOS | CFR relates regret minimization to approximate equilibrium for finite two-player zero-sum games with perfect recall; OOS extends sampled regret methods to online search. | Keep for a later concrete strategic failure or a small independently justified subgame. A general full-match information-set abstraction, regret store and convergence campaign would greatly exceed the basic-bot need. [CFR, §§2–3](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2007/file/08d98638c6fcd194a4b1e6992063e944-Paper.pdf). |
| Deep CFR | Approximates CFR with neural networks; the paper evaluates poker and describes its information-set/action representation. | Removes some tabular burden, not the need for traversal, valid features, training or evaluation. Not selected. [Brown et al., §§3–5](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/brown19b/brown19b.pdf). |
| ReBeL | Combines self-play and search over public belief states; its paper explicitly notes representation growth with possible private information states. Released work is for Liar's Dice, not the full poker system. | Valuable warning against copying AlphaZero into hidden information. No evidence of a cheap Netrunner implementation. [Brown et al., §§4–5, 9 and broader impact](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13544). |

For the proposed first search experiment, use simple composition constraints plus conservative uncertainty before learned beliefs. Weight hypotheses softly using observable play only if a measured opponent model justifies it. Bound sampling work; if no consistent particles can be produced, report the inconsistency and use the lawful baseline. Never recover by reading the true state. Aggressive rejection sampling can exhaust the decision budget, and blindly perturbing particles can violate remembered information. Both failures need diagnostics.

A short horizon must stop at a meaningful decision boundary or preserve pending effects, rather than truncate midway through a cost and call the result favorable. A value estimate at cutoff is an estimate, not a terminal result. Model the actual supported consequences through the common engine; do not create a cheaper approximate rules simulator and quietly treat its results as equivalent.

## Growing to many supported cards and decks

Hundreds of cards need not require one separate bot for every deck. They do require correct card effects, usable observations, manageable choices and relevant strategic knowledge. Separate three claims:

1. **Rules admission:** every included card and reachable interaction is supported by the engine and its selected sources.
2. **Policy admission:** the cards and mechanics supply meaningful action handling and tactical features, rather than only a generic legal fallback. Aim for these pieces to compose across any permitted deck built from the admitted pool; do not require a bespoke script or manual bot implementation for each deck combination.
3. **Strength evidence:** the bot has been evaluated on named deck combinations and opponents at a named budget.

Reject unsupported rules before setup and detect missing bot action/mechanic handling at its actual boundary. Mark unmeasured bot strength honestly. Named evaluation decks bound strength claims; they need not be the only compositions a later admitted pool can play. A deck made entirely of implemented cards can introduce interactions or strategic dependencies that were absent from each card's isolated tests. Neither a printing ID nor a text embedding executes or understands an unfamiliar effect. The current delivery remains the exact introductory lists; this direction does not add deckbuilding to it.

**Recommended representation:** score the current engine-supplied legal candidates, rather than allocate one fixed output neuron for every possible card, target, payment and choice combination. Candidate features can include known source/target properties, costs, server context, immediate consequences and role-specific tactical estimates. Represent visible cards and entities as collections; retain explicit order where order matters, such as ICE positions and known deck information. Distinguish unknown values from zero. Keep the authoritative rules in authored Rust, with small card-specific planning hooks only where actual examples need them.

If a compact learned scorer is later justified, it can consume a set or sequence of known entities plus candidate features and role context. Shared entity encoding with separate role heads is one option; two small independent models may be easier to diagnose. Neither is selected now. Do not create a universal effect ontology solely to feed a hypothetical model. Basic costs and subtypes omit timing, conditional abilities and interactions.

Two primary precedents support investigating that shape, with narrow limits. [Deep Sets, §2.2/Theorem 2 and §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.06114v3) studies permutation-invariant collection models; it does not establish that a learned card representation transfers to new effects. [DouZero, §§3.1 and 4.1–4.2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/zha21a/zha21a.pdf#page=3) scores encoded legal actions with history and separate role networks. Its fixed card-combination game differs from Netrunner's authored mechanics, and its training hardware is not this project's budget. Its feature for the deducible union of other hands must not be copied as access to actual hidden Netrunner hands.

Generalization tests must name their distance from training: fresh seeds in a familiar matchup; unseen compositions made from familiar supported cards; supported cards withheld from training; or entirely new mechanics requiring code changes. These are different claims. The first cannot establish the last. For future supported pools, use coherent held-out decks and interaction families, not random decision rows from the same matches. New mechanics can require new features and tactical logic; that is an expected admission cost, not a failure of a promised universal model.

## Completed games as evidence and improvement data

Keep the smallest record that serves reproduction and one concrete analysis. At match level, identify engine/rules/content and policy revisions, decks, gameplay chance basis, policy randomness, work limits and the actual terminal result or explicit interruption. At selected decision points, retain the actor's lawful observation/history prefix, offered choices, chosen action, elapsed time, work counters and useful scored alternatives. Engine-private reproduction material belongs to a separate diagnostic path that policies cannot query. A user-facing replay product is not required.

Analyze decisions **as they appeared then**. A later agenda reveal cannot retroactively make a previously uncertain run certain. A winning game may contain poor decisions; a losing game may contain the best available decision followed by bad luck. Winner labels can train a value estimate from lawful prefixes, but do not make every winner action a good imitation target. Never put subsequent cards, final hand contents, final outcome, real shuffle seed or privileged engine IDs into an earlier policy input.

For a candidate error, compare alternatives from the same lawful prefix over fresh plausible worlds and declared opponent policies. The actual realized world can explain what happened; it cannot alone establish which action had the best expected result before the hidden cards were known. Historical logs contain outcomes only for actions taken. Counterfactual values require additional modeling or interventions and remain conditional on those assumptions.

Prioritize a few actionable failure categories: missed immediate win; affordable defense omitted; unsupported choice; incorrect payment plan; repeated unproductive action; overconfident hidden-card inference; poor risk calibration; or latency exhaustion. Preserve uncertain disagreements instead of labeling every surprising play a blunder. Useful examples return to a bounded policy change, source-derived regression case, or proposed experiment. They do not automatically generate an endless repair queue.

A developer or LLM critique can propose a suspect decision for investigation. It is not an automatic reward label or correctness oracle. Source-derived tactical expectations, lawful sampled comparisons and independent complete-game evidence support different claims and must remain distinguishable.

For the initial path, avoid privileged critics and teachers entirely. The learning inputs and deployed actors should use the same lawful information boundary. An analyst may inspect private engine records to diagnose a leak or rules defect; that is separate from teaching an actor to exploit those records. Runtime policies remain frozen during a normal game; improvement happens in an explicitly selected offline experiment and ships as a new identified version. This does not propose persistent profiling of Jon or silent adaptation between his games.

**Learning evidence:** [DAgger, §3/Algorithm 3.1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a/ross11a.pdf#page=4) addresses the distribution shift caused by a learner visiting states absent from demonstrations by asking a teacher about learner-visited states. Replaying existing logs alone is not that algorithm, and copying a weak teacher does not manufacture expertise. [BCQ, §3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/fujimoto19a/fujimoto19a.pdf#page=2) identifies extrapolation error when offline value learning evaluates insufficiently covered actions. Its continuous-control results do not select a Netrunner algorithm; they support keeping logged evidence, model estimates and measured alternative continuations distinct.

## A proportionate improvement sequence

| Stage | Smallest useful experiment | Evidence needed before relying on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Both role policies complete real introductory games, including nested choices and restart. | Rules and information checks, representative failures, runtime budget and actual human use. |
| Search | Compare the baseline with one fixed-work tactical/short-plan search variant on the same inputs. | Improvement against frozen opponents and independent positions, with fairness intact and extra time accounted for. |
| Parameter tuning | Tune a small explicit set of economic, risk or plan-ranking parameters. | Held-out comparison with unchanged hand-selected parameters and equal candidate/evaluation effort. Keep a complete candidate record, not only the winning run. |
| Imitation or value fitting | Train a compact model from lawful prefixes and a specified teacher or terminal outcomes. | Held-out games and decks, deployment latency, comparison against the teacher/baseline, and data coverage outside familiar trajectories. |
| Small RL experiment | Address a measured weakness after throughput and representation are adequate. | Equal-budget baselines, separate training seeds, frozen opponent mixture, new final evaluation, model/optimizer checkpoint identity and rollback. |

These steps do not imply automatic progression. The least-complex change that resolves a measured weakness can be the final answer. A better heuristic may beat a learned component for this product's budget. Several hundred games can diagnose large failures and guide a modest parameter comparison; they do not establish that deep RL has enough experience to learn the game from scratch.

## Evaluation that can detect regressions

Evaluate **Corp and Runner separately**. Changing both at once can hide a weaker Runner behind a much weaker Corp. Keep the old baseline and a small frozen population with materially different plausible styles, such as aggressive central pressure, conservative development and remote-focused scoring. Later candidates can be added after a measured comparison; the current candidate must not define all of its own opposition.

[PSRO, §3/Algorithm 1 and §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1711.00832v2) supplies a primary precedent for responses to retained policy mixtures and examines overfitting to training partners. A few frozen opponents capture the immediate comparison need without adopting PSRO's oracle-training and mixture-optimization machinery.

Use the same declared seed/deck/opponent blocks for candidate-versus-incumbent comparisons within a role. Evaluate the other role separately with its own blocks. When comparing complete policy packages, include both seat assignments, but do not assume Netrunner is symmetric or that 50% is the correct target. Equal initial shuffles help pairing; diverging choices consume chance differently, so record the actual trajectories and avoid claiming identical later luck.

Report wins/losses and rules-defined terminal reasons, censored games, per-role differences, uncertainty, tactical failure counts, fairness failures and timing separately. Hidden-information violations are a veto, not a penalty that win rate can compensate for. Do not drop stuck games from the denominator and call the remainder the completion rate. A batch watchdog limit is an interrupted diagnostic result, not a new draw rule or a training reward for stalling.

Use game or paired seed block as the statistical unit, not the many correlated decisions inside it. Cluster paired comparisons accordingly. As a rough independent-binomial illustration near a 50% win rate, 100 games give about a ±10 percentage-point 95% normal-approximation margin; 400 give about ±5 points, from `1.96 × sqrt(0.25 / n)`. This is not the interval for a particular paired experiment. Use appropriate proportion intervals such as those described by [NIST §7.2.4.1](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm) for actual independent win proportions, particularly near the boundaries. Small improvements, deck/opponent subgroups and correlated runs require more care; “hundreds” is a budget description, not automatic statistical adequacy.

[Agarwal et al., §4.1/Figure 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.13264v4) show that few-run evaluation can give unreliable comparisons and under-cover uncertainty. Many matches from one trained checkpoint do not measure variation between independent training runs. Retain separate role results instead of adopting an unnecessary composite benchmark score.

Separate development seeds/positions, validation used for selection, and a fresh final comparison. Repeatedly checking the same holdout while changing candidates turns it into development data. Record the number of candidates and evaluation rounds, and stop at the accepted appetite. Ablate one contribution at a time: baseline, baseline plus search, then the same search with tuned or learned scoring. If all components and compute budgets change, attribute the result to the combined system rather than one favored mechanism.

The general warning is established by [Dwork et al., introduction and §4](https://arxiv.org/html/1506.02629v2); their special reusable-holdout mechanism is not needed here. More directly, [Eimer et al. (2023)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/eimer23a.html) find that RL hyperparameter selection can depend strongly on tuning seeds and recommend separating tuning from testing seeds. These results support a small disciplined comparison, not a new evaluation platform.

Self-play results are conditional on the opponent population. They do not demonstrate low exploitability against every strategy. A scalar rating can conceal a cycle in which A beats B, B beats C and C beats A. Preserve the matchup matrix and severe regressions. Exact exploitability for full Netrunner is not currently available; do not relabel a win rate or a shallow best-response test as that quantity.

Experienced-casual strength eventually needs independently credible human opposition or a validated benchmark at that level. Jon's play establishes enjoyment and usability for Jon; expert tactical cases establish selected tactical behavior. Each is useful, and neither alone establishes general full-game strength. This note does not authorize recruiting or messaging testers.

## Work, latency and failure bounds

Hard decisions have a **20-second ceiling**, not a target for every choice. Routine forced or obvious choices should finish quickly. Keep a legal baseline choice ready, run optional search away from the render thread, cancel on changed revision/load/exit, and validate the result again before applying it. Expensive individual operations also need cancellation boundaries; checking a deadline only between very long rollouts is insufficient.

Use fixed work for reproducible strength comparisons and separate wall-clock trials on Jon's machine. Count engine transitions, sampled worlds, completed/cutoff rollouts, branching, sampler rejection, cache hits, memory, decision latency and fallback reasons. A fixed simulation count alone is unfair if one algorithm spends much more time in feature extraction or inference. Report both work and actual elapsed cost.

The host inspection found an Intel i9-12900K with 24 logical CPUs, an RTX 2080 Ti with 11,264 MiB of VRAM, and approximately 30.6 GiB of system memory. This identifies the local constraint; it supplies no measured throughput. For scale only, **400 games × 200 decisions × 0.1 seconds** would take about **2.2 serial hours** before overhead; at one second per decision it would take about **22.2 hours**. Two hundred decisions is an illustrative assumption, not an observed match length. Expensive runtime search cannot simply be multiplied into a claim that hundreds of games fit overnight. Measure short baseline batches first; then decide whether to parallelize independent games, lower batch search budgets, or narrow the experiment. Label differing evaluation budgets explicitly.

A bounded batch needs a time/game/storage cap and a retained failure prefix. Detect repeated no-progress decisions, excessive automatic transitions, unresolved effects and policy timeouts. Preserve enough state to distinguish an engine defect, legal long game, bad policy and harness interruption. Do not invent progress rules that change Netrunner to make the job finish. An overnight authorization covers the specified job, not repeated nights or automatic tuning until a desired score appears.

## Small interfaces worth preserving

These are **proposed responsibilities**, not a schema or adopted API:

| Boundary | What crosses it | What stays outside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Controller → actor | Player observation, lawful new events/history, actor/revision and legal decision choices. | Full match/save, real chance state, another player's private data. |
| Actor → controller | Selected current choice plus bounded diagnostic metadata. | Authority to alter state, create legality or skip timing. |
| Lawful history → hypothetical sampler | Known composition/observations, declared prior and separate exploration seed. | Actual hidden card allocation and actual draw order. |
| Hypothetical simulator → simulated actor | That actor's simulated information and choices at each step. | Omniscient sample contents outside that actor's rights. |
| Batch analysis → candidate policy | Versioned parameter/code/model proposal with its evidence and limits. | Automatic promotion into ordinary play. |

Reuse the existing observation → revision-checked decision → transition seam. Keep policies local to Netrunner. The [sibling source audit](evidence.md#reuse-findings) identifies shared randomness and tooling mechanisms separately from game semantics. Arkham's agent-player hosting is not an existing offline Netrunner planner. Any necessary common tooling still follows the [shared-code workflow](../foundation/05-shared-code-and-development.md); this research does not select an extraction.

The immediate recommendation is therefore a basic lawful policy for each role and complete-match diagnostics, with exact tactical support where the introductory cards demand it. Information-aware search is the first plausible later improvement experiment. Training, generalization and larger-deck competence remain questions for measured, separately bounded work.
